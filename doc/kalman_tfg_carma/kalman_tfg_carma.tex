\begin{abstract}
This experimental paper moves the three-axis CARMA(4,1) wave-motion filter
CARMA3 from its quaternion multiplicative EKF (MEKF) onto a right-invariant
two-frame Lie group, keeping its CARMA prior, wrapper, startup, adaptation and
magnetic handling unchanged, and asks whether the coherent error geometry
improves estimation. The 21-state filter TFG-CARMA3 has estimate-independent
accelerometer and magnetometer Jacobians, a group injection and a full reset
transport; for a CARMA prior with one shape on every axis the attitude does
not enter the translational dynamics, and the structured impulse response of
the transition collapses to a closed form. The two filters are first-order
equivalent, and a paired simulation benchmark (36 conditions, 20--100 seeds,
plus 80 replays of an independent vessel-RAO dataset) confirms it for
translation: in 34 of 36 conditions every displacement and velocity change
lies inside $\pm1\,\%$ with its whole 95\,\% interval. Where the CARMA prior
misfits (a mixed swell and wind sea, manoeuvring) the invariant coordinates
give 2.6--6\,\% lower tilt error, 3--7\,\% lower accelerometer-bias error and
7.6--12\,\% lower attitude NEES, and through a magnetometer outage the filter
without reset transport keeps an honest heading covariance (NEES 1.2 over
\SI{2400}{s}). It costs 3.4 times CARMA3's multiply-adds. The results are
simulation only; nothing here changes the shipping OU-III estimator or bears
on its stability study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Status}
\label{sec:intro}
The experimental CARMA3 filter (\texttt{src/kalman\_carma}, companion paper
\texttt{kalman\_carma.pdf}) replaces the three integrated Ornstein--Uhlenbeck
chains of the shipping OU-III marine estimator by stationary CARMA(4,1)
displacement priors~\cite{brockwell01} on all three world axes. It keeps
OU-III's quaternion MEKF~\cite{markley03}: additive errors on the world-frame
CARMA states and the body-frame biases, and a body-frame attitude error. The
two-frame Lie-group filter of the repository (\texttt{kalman\_tfg.pdf}) carries
world states and body biases in one group with a right-invariant
error~\cite{barrau17,barrau18}. This paper combines the two and tests the
hypothesis
\begin{quote}\small
representing attitude, the CARMA translational states and the IMU biases on
the right-invariant two-frame group yields measurably better estimation
(accuracy, robustness, bias tracking or covariance consistency) than the
quaternion MEKF with the same three-axis CARMA(4,1) prior.
\end{quote}
Everything else is held fixed: the same CARMA model library, shape,
frequency/amplitude mapping and exact discretization; the same wrapper source
(front end, proxy startup, staged tune commit, tilt watchdog,
magnetometer-service bias gate, vibration-aware accelerometer covariance); the
same sensor streams and the same physical initial uncertainty.

\emph{Status.} TFG-CARMA3 is a research prototype. It is not included by the
deployed configuration, it has no on-target timing or hardware evidence, and
no result here is a stability, admissibility or hardware statement. The full
report, with every table, is \texttt{docs/tfg-carma3-research.md}; the
generated statistics are in \texttt{reports/results/tfg\_carma3}.

\section{State, Group and Error}
\label{sec:group}
The nominal state is $X=(R,W,B)$ in the two-frame group with four world and
two body columns,
\begin{gather}
R=R_{bw}\in\mathrm{SO}(3),\notag\\
W=[\,p\;v\;a\;l\,]\in\R^{3\times4},\quad
B=[\,b_g\;b_a\,]\in\R^{3\times2},
\end{gather}
where row $k$ of $W$ is the CARMA state of world axis $k$ in the library
coordinates (displacement, velocity, acceleration, latent), and the group law
is
\begin{equation}
(R_1,W_1,B_1)\circ(R_2,W_2,B_2)=(R_1R_2,\;W_1+R_1W_2,\;B_2+R_2^{\top}B_1).
\end{equation}
The error is right-invariant, $\eta=\hat X X^{-1}=\Exp_G(\xi)$, and corrections
act on the left, $\hat X^{+}=\Exp_G(-\hat\xi)\hat X$. In local coordinates
\begin{equation}
\Exp(\phi)=\hat R R^{\top},\quad \rho_i=\hat w_i-\Exp(\phi)\,w_i,\quad
\beta=R(\hat b-b),
\end{equation}
ordered as $\xi=[\phi\,|\,\beta_g\,|\,\rho_p\,\rho_v\,\rho_a\,\rho_l\,|\,\beta_a]$
(21 states). The attitude error is world-referred, and the body biases are
carried as world-referred vectors whose body-frame meaning is fixed by the
true attitude.

\section{Error Dynamics and Discretization}
\label{sec:dynamics}
With measured rate $\omega_m$, gyro white noise $n_g$, bias walk $n_{bg}$,
CARMA driving noise $\zeta\in\R^3$ with spatial covariance $\Sigma_s$ and the
residual accelerometer-bias OU process of CARMA3,
\begin{equation}
R'=R\Skew{\omega_m-b_g-n_g},\quad W'=WA^{\top}+g\,e_a\zeta^{\top},\quad
b_g'=n_{bg},
\end{equation}
and $b_a'=-b_a/\tau_b+n_{ba}$ (active) or $b_a'=0$ (held). $A$ is the
CARMA(4,1) realization at the committed frequency scale $\kappa$ with unit
acceleration variance, $g^2=\kappa/\mathrm{Var}_1(a)$. The mean propagation
over a step $h$ is exact: $R\leftarrow R\Exp(\omega h)$,
$W\leftarrow W\Phi_A^{\top}$ with $\Phi_A=e^{Ah}$.

Linearized at $\xi=0$, with $\omega_w=\hat R\omega$,
\begin{align}
\phi' &= -\beta_g+\hat R n_g,\qquad
\beta_g' = \Skew{\omega_w}\beta_g-\hat R n_{bg},\\
\rho_i' &= \textstyle\sum_j A_{ij}\rho_j-\Skew{\hat w_i}\beta_g
           +\Skew{\hat w_i}\hat R n_g-g\,e_{a,i}\,\zeta .
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[No attitude-to-translation term]
\label{prop:commute}
With one $A$ on every axis, $(QW)A^{\top}=Q(WA^{\top})$ for every rotation
$Q$, so the attitude error does not act on $W$ through $A$:
$\Phi_{\rho,\phi}=0$.
\end{proposition}

The $\beta_g\to\rho$ coupling $-\Skew{\hat w_i}$ depends on the estimate, so
the filter is a right-invariant two-frame error-state EKF, not a group-affine
(log-linear) invariant EKF; no estimate independence of the error dynamics is
claimed.

\begin{proposition}[Collapse of the impulse response]
\label{prop:collapse}
Since the skew map is linear in each column and
$\Phi_A(h-t)\Phi_A(t)=\Phi_A(h)$,
\begin{equation}
(\Phi_A(h-t)\otimes I_3)\,\Skew{W(t)}=\Skew{W(t)\Phi_A(h-t)^{\top}}
=\Skew{W(h)}
\end{equation}
for every $t$. Hence every $\rho$ column of the transition and of the noise
injection is $\Skew{w_i(h)}$ times the attitude column:
$\Phi_{\rho_i,b_g}=\Skew{w_i(h)}F$ with $F=-R_0hJ_l(\omega h)R_0^{\top}$.
\end{proposition}

The quadrature that the OU filter on the same group needs is therefore
unnecessary for this prior. After the collapse the discrete process noise
needs only $3\times3$ integrals: $Q_{\rho_i,\rho_j}=\Skew{w_i(h)}Q_{\phi\phi}
\Skew{w_j(h)}^{\top}+Q_A(i,j)\,\Sigma_s$, where $Q_A$ is the library's exact
CARMA process covariance at unit amplitude, so the CARMA part is
$Q_A\otimes\Sigma_s$ exactly. The prior itself is CARMA3's: the fitted
CARMA(4,1) JONSWAP shape with a zero at DC, the mapping $\kappa=f_{\mathrm{tune}}
/f_{z,\mathrm{model}}$ and the amplitude from the measured band acceleration.
There is no auxiliary $S$ state.

\section{Measurements, Correction and Reset}
\label{sec:measurement}
The accelerometer and magnetometer residuals are formed in the world frame,
$r_a=\hat R(f_m-\hat b_a-k_a\Delta T)-(\hat a-g)$ and $r_m=\hat Rm-B_w$, with
noise $\hat R\,R_{a,m}\hat R^{\top}$. At $\xi=0$
\begin{align}
H_a&=[\,\Skew{g}\;|\;0\;|\;0\;0\;-I\;0\;|\;-I\,],\notag\\
H_m&=[\,-\Skew{B_w}\;|\;0\;\cdots\,],
\end{align}
exactly: the Jacobians do not depend on the estimate. The world residual is
$\hat R$ times CARMA3's body residual, so the innovation information is
identical; only the error coordinates differ. CARMA3's semantics are kept: no
innovation gating, and a held accelerometer bias stays in the innovation
covariance but not in the gain.

The estimated error $\mathbf a=K r$ is injected by
$\hat X^{+}=\Exp_G(-\mathbf a)\hat X$, which rotates every world column and
re-expresses the biases. The Joseph-form posterior covariance is transported
into the new coordinates by the right Jacobian
\begin{equation}
J_r(\mathbf a)=\textstyle\sum_n(-\mathrm{ad}_{\mathbf a})^n/(n+1)!
\end{equation}
whose blocks are $J_l(-\phi)$ and the $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ left-Jacobian block
$Q_r(\phi,z)$~\cite{barfoot17} for each vector block $z$. Two ablations remove
part of it: attitude-only ($J_l(-\phi)$ on the attitude block, the MEKF
analogue) and none.

\section{Relation to the Quaternion MEKF}
\label{sec:equivalence}
CARMA3's errors $e=[\delta\theta_b,\delta b_g,\delta w,\delta b_a]$ map to
first order as
\begin{equation}
\phi=\hat R\,\delta\theta_b,\quad \beta=-\hat R\,\delta b,\quad
\rho_i=-\delta w_i+\Skew{\hat w_i}\hat R\,\delta\theta_b,
\end{equation}
a linear map $\xi=T(\hat X)e$ that depends on the estimate. Both linear models
are exact first-order linearizations of the same system, so
$\Phi_{\mathrm{TFG}}=T(\hat X_1)\Phi_{\mathrm{MEKF}}T(\hat X_0)^{-1}$,
$H_{\mathrm{TFG}}=\hat R H_{\mathrm{MEKF}}T^{-1}$ and
$K_{\mathrm{TFG}}=TK_{\mathrm{MEKF}}\hat R^{\top}$.

\begin{proposition}[First-order equivalence]
\label{prop:equiv}
The two filters differ only through the injection (equal to first order), the
covariance transport and the estimate at which $T$ is evaluated around a
correction. In CARMA3's coordinates the TFG update implies, to first order in
the correction,
$\delta w_i^{+}=\delta w_i-\tfrac12\Skew{\phi_a}\delta w_i
+\tfrac12\Skew{\Delta\hat w_i}\phi$, against CARMA3's
$\delta w_i^{+}=\delta w_i$.
\end{proposition}

The term is a product of a state correction and the attitude error. The
filters therefore separate only where the world states receive large
corrections, which is where the CARMA prior misfits.

\emph{Unobservable heading.} Without a magnetometer a rotation of the world
about the vertical is a symmetry. In the invariant coordinates the
unobservable direction is $e_{\phi_z}$ for every estimate
($H_ae_{\phi_z}=\Skew{g}e_z=0$, $\Phi e_{\phi_z}=e_{\phi_z}$), so the
information along it cannot grow under prediction or update. In CARMA3's
coordinates the direction $n(\hat X)=[\hat R^{\top}e_z,0,\Skew{\hat w}e_z,0]$
moves with the estimate, and each update adds information along the new
direction, the classical source of false heading
observability. The full reset $J_r(\mathbf a)$ re-introduces an estimate
dependence; only the filter without transport preserves the direction exactly.

\emph{Equivariance.} With one $A$ on every axis the CARMA transition commutes
with every world rotation and
$Q_d(Q\Sigma_sQ^{\top})=(I\otimes Q)Q_d(\Sigma_s)(I\otimes Q)^{\top}$. The
complete filter is yaw-equivariant for the isotropic horizontal prior that the
wrapper uses. Different horizontal and vertical CARMA models would add an
attitude-to-translation coupling; the implementation refuses such a
configuration rather than approximating it.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}
\texttt{Kalman3D\_Wave\_TFG\_CARMA3<T,NC>} stores the nominal state as a
\texttt{TwoFrameGroup<T,NC,2>} and a dense symmetric $21\times21$ covariance.
The prediction applies the structured transition in two passes and the
structured $Q_d$; the update forms $PH^{\top}$ from the sparse $H$, solves for
$K$, applies CARMA3's symmetric Joseph form, the structured
$J_r(\mathbf a)PJ_r(\mathbf a)^{\top}$, the group injection and CARMA3's
gyro-bias and accelerometer-bias mean projections. Every covariance-sized
temporary is a member: there is no dynamic allocation after construction and
no $21\times21$ stack temporary. The wrapper is the CARMA3 wrapper with its
inner-filter template parameter set; every covariance write CARMA3 performs in
its own coordinates (attitude seeds, the stationary CARMA prior, bias freeze,
heading gauge) is applied to $T^{-1}PT^{-\top}$ and mapped back, so both
filters represent the same physical uncertainty at every phase change.

\section{Paired Benchmark}
\label{sec:bench}
\texttt{carma3\_bench --extended} feeds one random-phase directional sea,
through the vessel RAOs of the CARMA study, and one IMU/magnetometer stream per
seed (\SI{200}{Hz}) identically to every filter. Errors are against the wave
coordinate in the geographic frame, scored from \SI{300}{s}. There are 100
paired seeds on the four principal seas, 30 on each of 29 further conditions
(broadband, bimodal, headings, anchored, drifting, underway, manoeuvring,
still IMU, small seas, large roll/pitch, rapid turns, magnetometer gaps, bias
and temperature drift, sample jitter) and 20 seeds of \SI{3600}{s} on three
more. The paired change is $1-\overline{\mathrm{TFG}}/\overline{\mathrm{MEKF}}$
(positive when TFG-CARMA3 is lower) with a 95\,\% bootstrap interval, the
Holm-corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank test and an equivalence statement when the
whole interval lies inside $\pm1\,\%$, a margin fixed before the runs.

\emph{Translation is equivalent.} In 34 of the 36 conditions every
displacement and velocity change lies inside $\pm1\,\%$ with its whole
interval, typically within $\pm0.01\,\%$ (\cref{tab:principal}). The two
exceptions are the \SI{900}{s} and \SI{2400}{s} magnetometer outages, whose

\emph{Attitude and bias are better where the prior misfits.} In the mixed
swell and wind sea tilt is 3.0\,\% lower (\SI{3600}{s}: 6.0\,\%) and the

yaw is 13.3\,\% lower ($[7.5,18.9]$, raw $p=7\times10^{-4}$, not significant

and bias are equivalent. The accelerometer-bias error is
17.1--17.4\,mg per degree of tilt error in every condition, against
$g\pi/180=17.45$\,mg/deg: the residual horizontal bias error is the
tilt-equivalent bias the accelerometer cannot separate from tilt.

mixed-sea heave, are unchanged to 0.02\,\%: they belong to the prior, not to
the error geometry.

\section{Pinned Vessel-RAO Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}
The eight scored records of the pinned 28\,ft vessel-RAO dataset are a second
simulator with its own sensor models. Over 80 paired replays (8 records,
10 seeds) the translational changes are +0.006\,\% (heave), $-$0.012\,\%
(surge) and $-$0.018\,\% (sway), with no replay differing by more than
0.11\,\%. Roll, pitch and heading change by +0.06\,\%, +0.29\,\% and +0.25\,\%
on average. CARMA3's advantage over OU-III is unchanged; for example, on
JONSWAP $H_s=\SI{1.5}{m}$ the heave error is 4.03\,\% of $H_s$ for OU-III
against 2.21\,\% for both CARMA filters.

\section{Mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanism}
\Cref{prop:equiv} explains the translational result: the filters share the
measurement information, the prior and the wrapper, and differ only by terms
that are products of a state correction and the attitude error. Where the
prior fits, both factors are small after convergence and the filters agree to
$10^{-4}$ in every translational RMS. All 32 conditions with a CARMA3
velocity error below \SI{0.045}{m/s} show tilt changes within $\pm0.5\,\%$.
The pair separates in the mixed sea and when manoeuvring, where the
single-peak prior misfits a bimodal sea or a low-frequency translation and the
acceleration state receives large corrections every update; the \SI{2400}{s}
magnetometer loss also has a large velocity error, from heading error rotating
the horizontal estimates, and an unchanged tilt. The magnitude of the motion
does not order the conditions.

The ablations locate the gain in the invariant coordinates. Writing the
covariance policies natively instead of physically changes nothing; removing
the reset transport \emph{increases} the mixed-sea tilt gain from 3.0\,\% to
4.6\,\% and the manoeuvring heading gain from 13.3\,\% to 23.6\,\%, but loses
the manoeuvring tilt gain. The interpretation consistent with all of this is
one property, the estimate dependence of the MEKF's error coordinates. For
tilt, CARMA3's accelerometer attitude Jacobian $\Skew{\hat R^{\top}(\hat a-g)}$
is evaluated at the CARMA acceleration estimate, so a misfitting prior enters
the attitude sensitivity, whereas $H_a$ of \cref{sec:measurement} does not
depend on the estimate. For heading, the moving unobservable direction admits
false information (\cref{sec:verify}). With CARMA(5,2), which misfits the
mixed sea more strongly, the MEKF's tilt error grows to \SI{1.64}{\degree}
(attitude NEES 819), the full-reset TFG's to \SI{0.99}{\degree} and the TFG
without transport keeps \SI{0.34}{\degree} (NEES 50), lower than the MEKF in
30 of 30 seeds. No experiment removed the estimate dependence from CARMA3's
MEKF itself, so this remains an interpretation.

The shared attitude overconfidence is mostly an accelerometer-bias effect: the
simulated turn-on bias reaches 8\,mg against the 0.4\,mg stationary deviation
of the inherited bias model, and with a calibrated accelerometer CARMA3's
attitude NEES falls from 48 to 23.

\section{Limitations and Recommendation}
\label{sec:recommend}
The limits are those of the evidence: simulation only, on one synthetic bench
and one replay dataset with the IMU at the centre of gravity; CARMA3's
policies mapped into the invariant coordinates rather than redesigned; a
shared attitude inconsistency; the unexplained end-of-run gyro-bias signal;
no group-affine property and no stability result; one CARMA shape and scale
on every axis; an open trade-off between the correct first-order reset
transport and the more consistent filter without it; and multiple-comparison
correction only within each table.

For the quantity the filter exists to estimate, wave displacement and
velocity, the two-frame group yields no measurable improvement over the
quaternion MEKF with the same prior: the filters are first-order equivalent
and the CARMA prior, not the error geometry, sets the translational error.
For attitude, heading consistency and the accelerometer bias it yields a
conditional improvement where the prior misfits, at 3.4 times CARMA3's
arithmetic. TFG-CARMA3 should not replace CARMA3's MEKF and should not be
deployed. The variant without reset transport merits a targeted continuation
against two falsifying experiments: an observability-constrained CARMA3 MEKF
(if it matches, the group is unnecessary) and on-target timing on the
ESP32-S3.

\section{Relation to the OU-III Stability Study}
The OU-III stability study and its premises (marine motion, IMU bias and
magnetic service, applied to one persistent physical execution) concern the
shipping OU-III filter. None of them transfers to this filter. A CARMA prior
is a statistical model, not a law imposed on the vessel's physical history,
and a replacement of OU-III would require its own process-model mismatch,
observability, covariance and full estimator stability analyses.
