in an OU-Driven Marine Inertial MEKF}

\begin{abstract}
A marine inertial multiplicative extended Kalman filter (MEKF) with a latent
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck (OU) acceleration state uses soft zero pseudo-measurements
on displacement and velocity to suppress low-frequency integration drift. This
paper derives the deployed sea-state scaling of those two regularizers from a
joint physical mean-square-error (MSE) objective. A reduced two-state
Kalman--Bucy algebraic Riccati equation admits a closed-form solution
parameterized by a position-regularization frequency and a dimensionless
velocity-to-position strength ratio. Combining residual-acceleration drift with
distortion of the physical displacement spectrum gives, in the weak-
regularization regime,
$r_p\propto q_{\rm eff}^{1/10}\sigma_a^{4/5}\tau^{12/5}T_S^{-1/2}$ and
$r_v\propto q_{\rm eff}^{1/10}\sigma_a^{4/5}\tau^{7/5}T_S^{-1/2}$, with
$r_p/r_v\propto\tau$. The deployed implementation evaluates these laws using
the measured acceleration scale, OU correlation time, and pseudo-update period,
with analytical constants obtained from the finite-band reference spectra. The
analysis explains the two-channel schedule without introducing an alternative
empirical adaptation law or a law-comparison ablation. Its principal limitation
is that attitude, bias, and axis-dependent gravity leakage are reduced to the
effective acceleration-error intensity $q_{\rm eff}$.
\end{abstract}

Kalman filter, inertial navigation, marine motion estimation,

\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Low-frequency drift is the central difficulty in displacement estimation from
inertial acceleration. The OU--II estimator considered here regularizes the
integration chain with two soft zero pseudo-measurements: one on world-frame
displacement $p$ and one on world-frame velocity $v$. Their configured standard
deviations are design variables rather than physical sensor uncertainties; they
trade drift suppression against attenuation of real marine motion.

Marine virtual-reference methods provide precedent for imposing nonphysical
constraints on inertial integration. Bryne, Fossen, and Johansen augmented a
marine INS observer with integrated vertical position and used a zero virtual
measurement motivated by the long-time mean of vessel heave; subsequent work
developed this into a virtual-vertical-reference aiding concept for low-cost
MEMS inertial sensing on surface vessels \cite{Bryne2014,Bryne2015,Bryne2018,Rogne2021}.
Recent heave and height estimators likewise combine inertial measurements with
explicit state-space filtering \cite{Reis2023,Overaas2025,Li2025}.

The question addressed here is narrower: how should the two OU--II
pseudo-measurement strengths scale as the sea changes? Dimensional consistency
alone cannot determine the powers of acceleration amplitude and sea period. A
physical design objective must account for both residual stochastic drift and
the distortion introduced by suppressing actual wave displacement. The
resulting law is the one deployed by OU--II; no historical non-analytic law is
used as a comparison arm in this paper.

\section{OU--II Translational Regularization}
\label{sec:implementation}

With gyroscope and accelerometer biases enabled, the complete OU--II MEKF has
local error coordinates containing attitude, gyroscope bias, velocity,
displacement, latent OU acceleration, and accelerometer bias. For the scaling
derivation, isolate the low-frequency translational chain
\begin{equation}
 \dot p=v,\qquad \dot v=n_a,
 \label{eq:double-integrator}
\end{equation}
where $n_a$ is the residual acceleration error presented to the integration
chain after acceleration estimation. Over the regularization band it is
approximated as white with two-sided intensity $q_{\rm eff}$, abbreviated $q$.

Let $r_p$ and $r_v$ denote the discrete standard deviations of the zero
pseudo-measurements, both applied with period $T_S$. Their continuous-equivalent
measurement-noise densities are
\begin{equation}
 \rho_p=r_p^2T_S,
 \qquad
 \rho_v=r_v^2T_S.
 \label{eq:continuous-densities}
\end{equation}
The deployed scheduler makes $T_S$ proportional to the applied OU correlation
time away from safety clamps. Consequently, the cadence must remain explicit
when mapping a continuous design optimum back to the discrete filter.

\section{Exact Dual-Channel Riccati Reduction}
\label{sec:care}

Consider
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\vct x}=\mat A\vct x+\mat B n_a,
 \qquad
 \vct x=\begin{bmatrix}p&v\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
 \mat A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 \mat B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 \mat Q=q\mat B\mat B^{\top}.
\end{equation}
The two continuous pseudo-observations have $\mat C=\Id$ and
$\mat R_c=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_p,\rho_v)$. The stabilizing covariance obeys
the Kalman--Bucy algebraic Riccati equation
\cite{KalmanBucy1961,Jazwinski1970,SarkkaSvensson2023,Anderson1971}
\begin{equation}
 \mat A\mat P+\mat P\mat A^{\top}+\mat Q
 -\mat P\mat R_c^{-1}\mat P=0.
 \label{eq:care}
\end{equation}

Define
\begin{equation}
 \omega_p:=\left(\frac{q}{\rho_p}\right)^{1/4},
 \qquad
 \omega_v:=\left(\frac{q}{\rho_v}\right)^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \chi:=\left(\frac{\omega_v}{\omega_p}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:natural-frequencies}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Dual-channel CARE solution]
For $q,\rho_p,\rho_v>0$, the stabilizing solution of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:care} is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mat P=\frac{q}{1+\chi}
 \begin{bmatrix}
 \dfrac{\sqrt{2+\chi}}{\omega_p^3} & \dfrac{1}{\omega_p^2}\\[2.5mm]
 \dfrac{1}{\omega_p^2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{2+\chi}}{\omega_p}
 \end{bmatrix}.}
 \label{eq:P-solution}
\end{equation}
The corresponding continuous pseudo-measurement gain is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mat K=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 \dfrac{\omega_p\sqrt{2+\chi}}{1+\chi} & \dfrac{\chi}{1+\chi}\\[2.5mm]
 \dfrac{\omega_p^2}{1+\chi} &
 \dfrac{\omega_p\chi\sqrt{2+\chi}}{1+\chi}
 \end{bmatrix}.}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The two pseudo channels therefore do not generate independent scalar corners;
they alter one second-order closed loop. If physical acceleration is injected
into the reduced dynamics, the acceleration-to-estimated-displacement transfer
is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 H_{pa}(s)=
 \frac{1}{1+\chi}
 \frac{1}{s^2+\omega_p\sqrt{2+\chi}\,s+\omega_p^2}.}
 \label{eq:Hpa}
\end{equation}
Relative to ideal double integration define $G(s):=s^2H_{pa}(s)$. A finite
velocity pseudo strength changes both low-frequency damping and the broadband
reconstruction gain, which is why the two regularizers must be designed jointly.

\section{Physical Displacement MSE}
\label{sec:mse}

Write the physical objective as
\begin{equation}
 J=J_n+J_w,
\end{equation}
where $J_n$ is the displacement variance generated by residual acceleration
error and $J_w$ is the distortion of the physical wave.

The exact reduced noise term is the squared $H_2$ response of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hpa},
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 J_n=
 \frac{q}{2\omega_p^3(1+\chi)^2\sqrt{2+\chi}}.}
 \label{eq:Jnoise-exact}
\end{equation}
For weak velocity regularization, $\chi\ll1$,
\begin{equation}
 J_n=
 \frac{q}{2\sqrt{2}\,\omega_p^3}
 \left(1-\frac94\chi+O(\chi^2)\right).
 \label{eq:Jnoise-expansion}
\end{equation}

Let $S_p^{(2)}(\omega)$ be the two-sided physical displacement spectrum and
define
\begin{equation}
 M_0:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 S_p^{(2)}(\omega)\,d\omega,
 \qquad
 M_{-2}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 \omega^{-2}S_p^{(2)}(\omega)\,d\omega.
\end{equation}
The wave-distortion term is
\begin{equation}
 J_w=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 |G(j\omega)-1|^2S_p^{(2)}(\omega)\,d\omega.
 \label{eq:Jwave-exact}
\end{equation}
When the regularization corner lies below the principal wave band and
$\chi\ll1$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{J_w\simeq M_0\chi^2+2M_{-2}\omega_p^2.}
 \label{eq:Jwave-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 J\simeq
 \frac{q}{2\sqrt{2}\,\omega_p^3}
 \left(1-\frac94\chi\right)
 +2M_{-2}\omega_p^2+M_0\chi^2.}
 \label{eq:J-reduced}
\end{equation}
The first contribution rewards stronger regularization by suppressing drift;
the latter two penalize distortion of the real motion.

\section{Joint MSE-Optimal Scaling}
\label{sec:optimal-scaling}

\begin{theorem}[Weak-regularization joint optimum]
The stationary point of Eq.~\eqref{eq:J-reduced} satisfies, to leading order,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 (\omega_p^\star)^5
 =\frac{3q}{8\sqrt{2}\,M_{-2}},}
 \label{eq:wp-opt}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \chi^\star
 =\frac{9q}
 {16\sqrt{2}\,M_0(\omega_p^\star)^3}.}
 \label{eq:chi-opt}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

For a fixed-shape, period-scaled physical acceleration spectrum, write
\begin{equation}
 S_a^{(2)}(\omega;\sigma_a,\tau)
 =\sigma_a^2\tau\,\Phi_a(\omega\tau).
\end{equation}
Since $S_p^{(2)}=S_a^{(2)}/\omega^4$, similarity gives
\begin{equation}
 M_0\propto\sigma_a^2\tau^4,
 \qquad
 M_{-2}\propto\sigma_a^2\tau^6.
 \label{eq:moment-scaling}
\end{equation}
Substitution into Eqs.~\eqref{eq:wp-opt}--\eqref{eq:chi-opt} yields
\begin{equation}
 \omega_p^\star
 \propto q^{1/5}\sigma_a^{-2/5}\tau^{-6/5},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \chi^\star
 \propto q^{2/5}\sigma_a^{-4/5}\tau^{-2/5},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \omega_v^\star
 \propto q^{2/5}\sigma_a^{-4/5}\tau^{-7/5}.
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:natural-frequencies},
\begin{equation}
 \rho_p^\star
 \propto q^{1/5}\sigma_a^{8/5}\tau^{24/5},
 \qquad
 \rho_v^\star
 \propto q^{1/5}\sigma_a^{8/5}\tau^{14/5}.
\end{equation}
Combining these densities with Eq.~\eqref{eq:continuous-densities} gives the
discrete filter-input laws
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 r_p^\star
 \propto q^{1/10}\sigma_a^{4/5}\tau^{12/5}T_S^{-1/2},}
 \label{eq:rp-opt}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 r_v^\star
 \propto q^{1/10}\sigma_a^{4/5}\tau^{7/5}T_S^{-1/2}.}
 \label{eq:rv-opt}
\end{equation}
Away from the pseudo-cadence clamps, $T_S\propto\tau$, so the effective filter
input powers are $r_p\propto\sigma_a^{4/5}\tau^{19/10}$ and
$r_v\propto\sigma_a^{4/5}\tau^{9/10}$ when $q$ is approximately independent of
sea amplitude to leading order.

\begin{corollary}[Optimal pseudo-channel ratio]
At the weak-regularization optimum,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \left(\frac{r_p^\star}{r_v^\star}\right)^2
 =\frac{3}{2}\frac{M_{-2}}{M_0}.}
 \label{eq:ratio-exact}
\end{equation}
For a fixed normalized sea shape,
$M_{-2}/M_0\propto\tau^2$, hence
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\frac{r_p^\star}{r_v^\star}\propto\tau.}
 \label{eq:ratio-tau}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The ratio is especially useful because $q$ and the common cadence cancel. It
sets the relative position/velocity regularization directly from physical
spectral moments rather than from two independently fitted channel gains.

\section{Deployed PhysicalMSE Schedule}
\label{sec:deployed}

The OU--II implementation evaluates Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rp-opt}--\eqref{eq:rv-opt}
as
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 r_p=
 C_P q_{\rm eff}^{1/10}\widehat\sigma_{a,B}^{4/5}
 \tau^{12/5}T_S^{-1/2},}
 \label{eq:deployed-rp}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 r_v=
 C_V q_{\rm eff}^{1/10}\widehat\sigma_{a,B}^{4/5}
 \tau^{7/5}T_S^{-1/2},}
 \label{eq:deployed-rv}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
 C_P=0.1116,
 \qquad
 \frac{C_P}{C_V}=0.4611,
 \qquad C_V\simeq0.2420.
 \label{eq:deployed-constants}
\end{equation}
The physical band-amplitude estimate $\widehat\sigma_{a,B}$ is recovered from
the scheduled OU prior amplitude rather than treating the prior coefficient as
an additional physical exponent. The residual acceleration intensity uses the
same measured acceleration-noise floor available to the tuner,
\begin{equation}
 q_{\rm eff}=2\sigma_{\rm floor}^2h,
 \qquad
 \sigma_{\rm floor}=\SI{0.12}{m.s^{-2}},
 \qquad
 h=\SI{0.005}{s}.
 \label{eq:deployed-qeff}
\end{equation}
The pseudo-update period is scheduled from the OU time scale and bounded for
implementation safety. Because $T_S$ appears explicitly in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:deployed-rp}--\eqref{eq:deployed-rv}, no additional historical
cadence renormalization is applied to the PhysicalMSE target.

The constants are obtained from the finite-band reference spectra through the
same moment relations used in the derivation. Across those spectra the inferred
ratio $C_P/C_V$ lies near the ideal fixed-shape values; the deployed value is a
single analytical reference for the common scheduler rather than a scenario-
specific fit.

\section{Interpretation}

Three structural conclusions follow. First, both pseudo channels depend on the
same physical acceleration amplitude with exponent $4/5$. Second, their period
powers differ by exactly one, so the position-to-velocity strength ratio scales
linearly with the sea time scale. Third, the common pseudo-update cadence enters
as $T_S^{-1/2}$ because the design is expressed in continuous-equivalent
measurement density and then mapped back to discrete updates.

These properties separate the roles of the three sea-dependent quantities:
$\widehat\sigma_{a,B}$ sets the physical wave amplitude scale, $\tau$ sets the
wave/OU time scale, and $T_S$ sets the rate at which the virtual constraints are
applied. The resulting schedule is therefore portable across seas without
reinterpreting the pseudo measurements as physical observations.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The derivation is a low-frequency reduced model. It assumes that the residual
acceleration error entering the integration chain can be approximated as white
over the regularization band and that the pseudo-measurement corner lies below
the dominant physical wave band. The closed-form optimum uses the
$\chi\ll1$ weak-distortion expansion; the exact transfer
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hpa} remains valid outside that approximation, but the power-law
optimum need not.

The displacement objective omits explicit attitude, magnetic, gyroscope-bias,
and accelerometer-bias penalties. In the full MEKF, both pseudo measurements act
through covariance cross-couplings, and the velocity channel can alter attitude
and bias estimation. A scalar $q_{\rm eff}$ also suppresses the axis dependence
created by gravity leakage. These effects can shift the quantitative optimum,
particularly horizontally, without changing the reduced-model derivation of
the deployed sea-scaling law.

\section{Conclusion}

A joint physical-MSE analysis of the OU--II displacement and velocity zero
pseudo-measurements gives a closed-form reduced Riccati model and the deployed
adaptation schedule. The discrete pseudo standard deviations scale as
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 r_p\propto q_{\rm eff}^{1/10}\sigma_a^{4/5}\tau^{12/5}T_S^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 r_v\propto q_{\rm eff}^{1/10}\sigma_a^{4/5}\tau^{7/5}T_S^{-1/2}.}
\end{equation}
Their ratio is fixed by physical spectral moments and satisfies
$r_p/r_v\propto\tau$. This construction derives the OU--II regularizer from a
physical displacement objective and supplies the analytical PhysicalMSE law
used by the current filter. Historical non-analytic adaptation laws and their
numerical comparisons are outside the publication scope.
