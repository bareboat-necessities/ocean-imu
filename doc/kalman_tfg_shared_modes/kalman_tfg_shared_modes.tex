\begin{abstract}
Directional wave motion and attitude error geometry are different sources of
structure in an inertial estimator. We combine the existing six-state minimal
shared-mode CARMA(4,1) realization on each spatial axis with a right-invariant
two-frame Lie-group filter, obtaining 27 error states. The implementation shares
its motion realization, exact discrete covariance pair, spectral adaptation,
coupling confidence and fallback with the quaternion multiplicative EKF (QMEKF),
and shares its prediction, sensor updates and group correction with the existing
TFG estimator. We derive the imaginary cross-axis process covariance and the
attitude shear required when adaptive latent coordinates are rescaled by axis.
A paired $2\times2$ experiment separates stochastic-prior effects from geometry
effects using \PrimarySeeds\ independent seeds in \ScenarioCount\ conditions,
with additional diagnostic ablations. \AbstractResults
This is simulation evidence for an experimental estimator, with explicit
finite-reset, consistency and hardware-validation limitations.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}marine inertial estimation, two-frame group, CARMA,
Hermitian excitation, adaptive filtering, paired Monte Carlo\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Motivation and Question}
Wave displacement about a local equilibrium is inferred from specific force,
angular rate and magnetic field. It is not geographic position: constant
translation, ocean current and a permanent displacement offset are not
observable from these measurements. A stationary stochastic wave prior can
regularize displacement without an artificial integral-displacement
measurement. Shared complex excitation can also express the phase relationships
between surge, sway and heave. Separately, the two-frame group (TFG) represents
world vectors and body biases in one group structure \cite{barrau}.
Neither observation implies that their accuracy improvements add.

The research question is whether changing the error geometry improves the
\emph{existing shared-mode QMEKF}, after holding data, tuning, startup and
coupling policy fixed. The primary endpoints are heave and horizontal position
RMS. A change below 5\% is treated as practically small, even when a paired
interval excludes zero. We also test the interaction between geometry and prior.
The shipping OU-III filter and its default selection are unchanged.

\section{Stochastic Motion and the 27-State Group}
\subsection{The CARMA(4,1) marginal law}
For each axis the fitted displacement transfer is
\begin{equation}
 H_p(s)=g_c\frac{s+\omega_z}
 {(s^2+2\zeta_1\omega_1s+\omega_1^2)
  (s^2+2\zeta_2\omega_2s+\omega_2^2)} .
 \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
The repository's JONSWAP shape has base frequencies
$f_1=0.306945$ Hz, $f_2=0.815318$ Hz, damping ratios
$\zeta_1=0.104191$, $\zeta_2=0.478188$, and $\omega_z=0$.
All poles are stable. The zero at DC and the stable displacement model bound
the displacement variance; the acceleration is not blindly double integrated.
The coefficients come from the existing offline, band-limited spectral fitting
procedure, not a fit to the test trajectories. Online adaptation changes
frequency and amplitude scales, not all polynomial coefficients.

One real four-state realization uses $u=(p,v,a,l)\T$, $l=r$ in the cascade
\begin{align}
 \ddot r+2\zeta_2\omega_2\dot r+\omega_2^2 r&=\xi,\\
 \ddot p+2\zeta_1\omega_1\dot p+\omega_1^2 p&=\dot r+\omega_z r.
\end{align}
Let $d=\omega_z-2\zeta_2\omega_2$. Eliminating $\dot r$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \resizebox{.98\columnwidth}{!}{$A_4=\begin{bmatrix}
 0&1&0&0\\0&0&1&0\\
 d\omega_1^2&2d\zeta_1\omega_1-\omega_1^2&d-2\zeta_1\omega_1&-\omega_2^2-d\omega_z\\
 \omega_1^2&2\zeta_1\omega_1&1&-\omega_z
 \end{bmatrix}$}
 \label{eq:a4}
\end{equation}
Frequency scaling is $\omega_i=2\pi\kappa f_i$. If $V_{a,1}$ is the
unit-gain base acceleration variance, the unit-acceleration driving intensity
is $g_c^2=\kappa/V_{a,1}$. Per-axis target acceleration standard deviations
are carried by the spatial excitation, so all axes have the same dynamics.

\subsection{Minimal shared modes}
A complex directional mode carries both in-phase and quadrature relationships.
The existing realization factors the upper-half-plane pole polynomial $P_+$
and a real Hurwitz spectral factor $D$, with
\begin{equation}
 |D(i\omega)|^2=\tfrac12\bigl(|P_+(i\omega)|^2+|P_+(-i\omega)|^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Its real co-inner coloring filter has two states $w=(w_1,w_2)\T$,
input-normal matrices $(A_w,B_w,C_w)$ and feedthrough $(1,0)$:
\begin{equation}
 A_w+A_w\T+B_wB_w\T=0,\qquad C_w=-e_1\T B_w\T .
\end{equation}
The realization uses an upper-triangular $B_w$ with positive diagonal to
fix its orthogonal coordinate freedom. In polynomial terms,
$P_+(s)=s^2+b_1s+b_0$ has the two upper-half-plane poles, and the co-inner
transfer is $[s^2+\Rea b_1s+\Rea b_0,\;\Ima b_1s+\Ima b_0]/D(s)$,
with coefficientwise real and imaginary parts. At scale $\kappa$,
the six-state realization and two real noise inputs are
\begin{align}
 A_6&=\begin{bmatrix}A_4&\sqrt\kappa\,e_a C_w\\0&\kappa A_w\end{bmatrix},&
 G_6&=\begin{bmatrix}e_a e_1\T\\\sqrt\kappa B_w\end{bmatrix},
 \label{eq:a6}
\end{align}
where $e_a=(0,0,1,0)\T$ and $A_w,B_w,C_w$ here are the unit-scale matrices.
This is the repository's existing minimal realization: the original eight
real coordinates contain two blind directions per spatial axis; eliminating
them leaves six. The marginal process remains CARMA(4,1). Six stored real
states do not make it CARMA(6,3). Continuous state-space reasoning is consistent
with the MCARMA framework, including its published correction \cite{schlemm,stelzer}.
No realization or factorization is independently reimplemented in the TFG core.

\subsection{State and error convention}
The estimator carries
\begin{align}
 X&=(R,W,B),& B&=[b_g,b_a],\\
 W&=[p,v,a,l,w_1,w_2]\in\R^{3\times6}.
\end{align}
$R$ rotates body to NED; $b_g,b_a$ are body-frame biases. The group law is
\begin{align}
 R_{12}&=R_1R_2,& W_{12}&=W_1+R_1W_2,\\
 B_{12}&=B_2+R_2\T B_1.
\end{align}
This is $\operatorname{TwoFrameGroup}(3,6,2)$: three attitude errors,
18 world-column errors and six bias errors, hence 27. The error is right
invariant, $\eta=\hat X X^{-1}=\Exp_G(\xi)$, and corrections act on the left:
$\hat X^+=\Exp_G(-\hat\xi)\hat X^-$. Tangent coordinates are
\begin{equation}
 \xi=(\phi,\beta_g,\rho_p,\rho_v,\rho_a,\rho_l,\rho_{w_1},\rho_{w_2},\beta_a),
\end{equation}
with $\delta R=\hat R R\T$, $\rho_i=\hat w_i-\delta Rw_i$ and
$\beta=R(\hat b-b)$ to first order. The group logarithm uses the SO(3)
Jacobian on vector slots; it is not just a componentwise subtraction.

\section{Invariant Prediction and Process Noise}
With measured rate $\omega_m$, $\omega=\omega_m-\hat b_g$ and
$\omega_W=\hat R\omega$, the nominal deterministic prediction is
\begin{align}
 \dot R&=R\sk{\omega}, &\dot W&=WA_6\T, &
 \dot b_a&=-b_a/\tau_b,
\end{align}
with accelerometer bias held when the common schedule disables its updates.
The linearized invariant equations are
\begin{align}
 \dot\phi&=-\beta_g+\hat Rn_g,\\
 \dot\beta_g&=\sk{\omega_W}\beta_g-\hat Rn_{bg},\\
 \dot\rho_i&=\sum_j A_{6,ij}\rho_j-\sk{\hat w_i}\beta_g
             +\sk{\hat w_i}\hat Rn_g-n_{c,i},\\
 \dot\beta_a&=(\sk{\omega_W}-\tau_b^{-1}I)\beta_a-\hat Rn_{ba}.
 \label{eq:continuous}
\end{align}
One $A_6$ on all axes commutes with rotation, cancelling the
attitude-to-motion deterministic term. Bias-to-motion terms still depend on
the estimated columns: this is an invariant-coordinate error-state EKF,
not a claim of exact autonomous log-linear error dynamics.

The exact motion transition is $F_c(h)=e^{A_6h}$. Defining
$E=R_1R_0\T$, $F_b=-R_0hJ_l(\omega h)R_0\T$ and
$W_1=W_0F_c\T$, the transition blocks are
\begin{align}
 \Phi_{\phi,b_g}&=F_b,&\Phi_{b_g,b_g}&=E,\\
 \Phi_{\rho_i,\rho_j}&=F_{c,ij}I,&
 \Phi_{\rho_i,b_g}&=\sk{w_{1,i}}F_b,\\
 \Phi_{b_a,b_a}&=e^{-h/\tau_b}E .
\end{align}
The identity $F_c(h-t)W(t)\T=W(h)\T$ collapses the gyro impulse response:
its motion noise columns are $\sk{w_{1,i}}$ times the attitude columns.
Consequently $Q_{\rho_i,\phi}=\sk{w_{1,i}}Q_{\phi\phi}$,
$Q_{\rho_i,b_g}=\sk{w_{1,i}}Q_{\phi b_g}$ and the gyro contribution to
$Q_{\rho_i,\rho_j}$ is
$\sk{w_{1,i}}Q_{\phi\phi}\sk{w_{1,j}}\T$.
These correlations are retained, not replaced by a standalone motion covariance.
The existing five-point quadrature computes the small bias/noise integrals;
structured two-pass covariance propagation avoids a dense $27^3$ product.

\subsection{Hermitian spatial excitation}
Write $\Sigma=S_R+iS_I\succeq0$, with $S_R=S_R\T$ and
$S_I=-S_I\T$. For
$J_2=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ define
\begin{align}
 D_R&=g_c^2G_6G_6\T,&D_I&=g_c^2G_6J_2G_6\T,\\
 Q_R(h)&=\int_0^h e^{A_6t}D_Re^{A_6\T t}\dd t,\\
 Q_I(h)&=\int_0^h e^{A_6t}D_Ie^{A_6\T t}\dd t .
\end{align}
The $3\times3$ spatial block between motion columns $i,j$ is exactly
\begin{equation}
 Q^{\mathrm{motion}}_{ij}=Q_R(i,j)S_R+Q_I(i,j)S_I.
 \label{eq:hermitian}
\end{equation}
The paired real noises have covariance
$S_R(k,l)I_2+S_I(k,l)J_2$ between spatial axes $k,l$; the convention gives
unit variance to each real channel when $\Sigma=I$.
$Q_R$ is symmetric and $Q_I$ antisymmetric, so the complete covariance is
symmetric. Omitting the imaginary term discards quadrature coupling even
when every per-axis spectrum still looks correct. The code reuses the shared
pair discretizer, including its local variable-step series and scaling/doubling
fallback. Dense continuous Lyapunov integration provides an independent
numerical reference. The real-only ablation keeps the same six-state dynamics
and maps only the co-spectrum, so $S_I=0$ and the cap below acts on the
coupling that is actually applied.

The excitation is formed from the band cross-spectral matrix $\hat S$ that the
coupling estimator measures around the operating frequency. With horizontal
prior $\sigma_h=S_f\sigma_z$ ($S_f=1$) it keeps the vertical prior and the
measured regression of horizontal on vertical motion,
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}H&b\\b^{\mathsf H}&\sigma_z^2\end{bmatrix},\quad
 b=s\,\kappa\,\frac{\sigma_z^2}{\hat S_{zz}}\hat S_{hz},\quad H=\sigma_h^2I_2,
 \label{eq:excitation-map}
\end{equation}
with $s$ the debiased-coherence shrinkage and $\kappa$ the applied coupling
confidence. For $s=\kappa=1$ its applied multiple coherence is
\begin{equation}
 q=\frac{b^{\mathsf H}H^{-1}b}{\sigma_z^2}
  =\gamma^2\,\frac{\hat S_{hh}/\hat S_{zz}}{\sigma_h^2/\sigma_z^2},
\end{equation}
which grows when the hull's horizontal-to-vertical amplitude ratio exceeds the
prior's, for example under a weak heave RAO. Positive semidefiniteness needs
$q\le1$. Scaling $b$ down to $q=0.999$, the legacy mapping, understates the
regression and leaves a singular prior that explains all horizontal motion
through heave. The shared estimators instead keep $b$ and, when $q>\rho$,
enlarge the horizontal block, $H\leftarrow(q/\rho)H$. The applied coherence is
then $\rho$, $\Sigma\succeq0$, and at least $(1-\rho)H$ of horizontal
innovation remains. One $\Sigma$ serves the whole spectrum while $\gamma^2$ is
measured in the band, so $\rho=0.8$ is a broadband target below the band
coherence. It was chosen on three development seeds; targets 0.7 and 0.9 are
reported on held-out seeds. The horizontal latent colouring is rescaled by
$\sqrt{q/\rho}$ in the same exact re-coordinatization as an amplitude commit,
and where the cap does not bind the mapping is unchanged.

\section{Measurements and Finite Corrections}
Specific force and magnetic measurements obey
\begin{align}
 f_m&=R\T(a-g)+b_a+k_a(T-T_0)+n_a,\\
 m_m&=R\T B_W+n_m.
\end{align}
There is no $S=0$ measurement or other artificial integral state. The inherited
accelerometer residual is world referred,
$r_a=\hat R(f_m-\hat b_a-k_a\Delta T)-(\hat a-g)$, and
$r_m=\hat Rm_m-B_W$. Its invariant Jacobians are
\begin{align}
 H_a&=[\sk g\;0\;0\;0\;-I\;0\;0\;0\;-I],\\
 H_m&=[-\sk{B_W}\;0\;\cdots\;0].
\end{align}
Noise is rotated by $\hat R$ into the same residual frame. The inherited
lever-arm option subtracts rotational specific force and adds its existing
gyro-bias Jacobian. Bias projection, held-bias semantics and magnetometer
semantics are unchanged; no new innovation gate is introduced.

The gain and Joseph update use the same sensor residual and covariance.
For $a=Kr$, the Joseph covariance is transported by $J_r(a)$, then the
nominal state is corrected by $\Exp_G(-a)$, using
\begin{align}
 P^+&=J_r(a)P^{\mathrm{Joseph}}J_r(a)\T,\\
 J_r(a)&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}
 \Log_G\!\left(\Exp_G(-a)\Exp_G(a+\epsilon)\right)\Big|_0 .
\end{align}
The full right Jacobian transports every world vector and both biases. A
research-only no-transport ablation sets this matrix to identity; it is not the
new filter's default.

\section{Adaptation, Confidence and Startup}
\subsection{Shared causal schedule}
The CARMA wrapper, including its private measurement-only attitude proxy,
period estimator, sensor-noise-aware Evidence excitation, staged model commits,
vibration conditioning, magnetic acquisition and tilt watchdog, is reused
without a second TFG-specific adaptation path. Excess band energy, its
uncertainty and frequency confidence determine wave excitation; the Legacy
noise floor is not used in any comparison cell. Commit cadence is 0.1 s.
The slower complex cross-spectrum estimates directional coherence and phase
from measured world acceleration reconstructed with the current attitude and
bias. No truth, vessel RAO or prescribed wave direction enters the operational
core.

\subsection{Adaptive coordinate changes}
The input-normal coloring coordinates scale with
$s_k=\sigma_{a,k}\sqrt{g_c^2}$. A commit changes the last two latent states by
$M_k=\diag(1,1,1,1,s_{k,\mathrm{new}}/s_{k,\mathrm{old}},
s_{k,\mathrm{new}}/s_{k,\mathrm{old}})$; $p,v,a,l$ retain their physical
mean values. Simply applying these axis maps to invariant covariance rows is
incorrect when the maps differ between axes.

For an axis map $\hat w'_{i,k}=\sum_jM_k(i,j)\hat w_{j,k}$,
the invariant linearization contains
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \rho'_{i,k}={}&\sum_jM_k(i,j)\rho_{j,k}\\
 &+\left(\bigl(\sk{\hat w'_i}\bigr)_{k,:}
 -\sum_jM_k(i,j)\bigl(\sk{\hat w_j}\bigr)_{k,:}\right)\phi .
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:shear}
\end{equation}
The implementation transports covariance into physical additive coordinates,
applies the existing maps to the mean and covariance, then maps back at the
new nominal state. This evaluates \cref{eq:shear} without a covariance-sized
stack temporary. A central finite difference of the complete nonlinear map
tests the attitude shear, including nonzero latent means and unequal axis scales.

\subsection{Confidence and fallback}
The same confidence state machine used by the shared QMEKF monitors band
regression mismatch, effective sample size, signal-to-noise ratio, innovation
change and maneuvering. Its hysteresis and recovery smoothing attenuate the
applied coupling gain. If confidence falls, let $r$ denote attitude and bias
errors, and partition motion into horizontal $h$ and vertical $z$. The fallback
changes only
\begin{equation}
 P_{hz}\leftarrow E+\alpha(P_{hz}-E),\quad
 E=P_{hr}P_{rr}^{-1}P_{rz},\quad 0\leq\alpha<1.
\end{equation}
The conditional covariance becomes
$C\leftarrow\alpha C+(1-\alpha)\diag(C_{hh},C_{zz})$.
Thus it preserves positive semidefiniteness in exact arithmetic and keeps
uncertainty explained by common attitude and bias states. Conditioning removes
the invariant attitude shear, so the same indexed Schur algorithm works in the
TFG column-major and QMEKF axis-major layouts. A failed positive factorization
leaves the covariance untouched. Both implementations call the extracted common
algorithm.

\subsection{Startup and magnetic requirements}
The proxy initializes tilt while the vessel may already be moving. The common
wrapper acquires a magnetic reference and transfers attitude to the live filter;
there is no startup truth reset in the experiments. A horizontal magnetic
component is required for heading information. During an outage, gravity and
wave structure alone do not supply absolute yaw; a simultaneous yaw rotation of
$R,W$ leaves predicted specific force unchanged. The simulation measures outage
uncertainty and heading recovery rather than assuming continued observability.
A tilt error can also mimic horizontal wave acceleration at scale
$g\,\delta\theta$, or be traded against an accelerometer bias. Magnetic
observations supply a direction independent of the instantaneous specific
force and help separate tilt from motion, but do not remove every bias/motion
gauge. Field inclination and vessel excitation still matter. Magnetic
anomalies and field-reference error remain limitations of the inherited
measurement policy.

\section{Verification and Regression Control}
The baseline revision is \texttt{1fe4711}; all runs use the same pinned host
compiler and Eigen installation. The existing TFG core passes 199 mathematical
checks and the existing CARMA QMEKF passes 74 before the refactor.
The independent 27-state references cover group inverse/composition/logarithm,
nonlinear prediction finite differences, continuous-time RK4 transition and
Lyapunov covariance integration, sensor Jacobians, dense Joseph updates,
finite-correction right Jacobians, adaptive coordinate maps, Schur fallback,
yaw equivariance and the outage yaw gauge. Double precision is used for these
references, with float for variable-step adaptive/outage execution and
allocation guarding. Structured 27-state propagation is checked against a
dense transition/covariance reference, including steps inside the cached
Taylor neighborhood. The yaw-equivariance test rotates state, covariance,
excitation and magnetic reference consistently with isotropic horizontal
amplitudes; a hull rotation under a fixed magnetic field is instead a
changed physical experiment, included among the Monte Carlo scenarios. \VerificationResults

At zero coupling, the observable stationary covariance and lag covariance
agree with the four-state CARMA process to numerical precision. This does
\emph{not} imply bitwise agreement of the nonlinear EKFs after finite group
corrections. The additional coloring-state covariance is also transported by
$J_r$; a Gaussian tangent approximation in two different latent dimensions can
therefore differ at second order. In a deliberately inconsistent 15 s stress
input (fixed magnetic vector while prescribing rotation), maximum position
difference is 0.342 mm. This diagnostic is explicitly reported rather than
misrepresented as a process-law failure or hidden by a changed existing gate.
The physically consistent full-wrapper coupling-off ablation is the relevant
operational comparison. \RegressionResults

\section{Controlled Comparative Experiment}
\begin{table}[!t]\centering\caption{Factorial design. All CARMA cells use Evidence excitation.}

The existing directional spectral synthesis produces physically consistent
position, velocity, acceleration, attitude and angular rate. Every filter in a
run receives the same seeded sensor stream, time intervals, calibration error
and magnetic service. JONSWAP and PM spectra, regular waves, swell, mixed seas,
wave heights, peak periods, spreading and vessel orientations vary across
\ScenarioCount\ scenarios. Explicit response-amplitude operators distinguish
incident elevation from vessel heave; reduced heave is tested separately.
Further conditions cover calm and low-energy water, four engine speeds,
colored accelerometer noise, frequency drift, sea-state growth and decay,
three-second direction changes, maneuvers, rapid rotations, gyro/accelerometer
bias drift and temperature, magnetic outage/reacquisition, and 25\% interval
jitter. Every non-calm trajectory starts in motion.

There are \PrimarySeeds\ independent primary seeds per scenario, each with
1200 s at 200 Hz; the primary score window is 300--1200 s. Startup errors and
convergence use their own earlier windows. The low--high--low scenario scales
wave amplitude from $0.5$ to $4$ at 400 s and back to $0.5$ at 800 s, using
20 s smooth transitions and differentiated physical kinematics.
The test sea is not generated by the estimator's CARMA law.

Ablations use \AblationSeeds\ independent seeds in six challenging scenarios:
coupling off; estimated coupling with confidence; a truth-derived band coupling
reference with confidence disabled; the same reference with quadrature removed;
estimated real-only coupling for both geometries; no covariance-reset transport;
and reversed coupling with and without confidence. Truth-derived references are
diagnostic inputs only. They are not an operational model or a guaranteed best
value under a frequency-dependent RAO mismatch.

\subsection{Metrics and statistics}
Per-run summaries use all 200 Hz samples, not rendering traces. We report heave,
horizontal and 3-D position RMS; velocity RMS; roll, pitch, tilt and heading;
sensor-bias RMS; accelerometer NIS; common physical and native joint NEES;
position coverage; startup, turn and outage recovery; nonfinite/divergence
flags; covariance symmetry and the minimum correlation-scaled eigenvalue at
one-second audits. A negative eigenvalue within float rounding is reported,
not silently converted into positive-definite evidence.

All comparisons pair the same seed and condition. For difference $d_s$,
$\bar d\pm t_{n-1,.975}\operatorname{sd}(d_s)/\sqrt n$ is the marginal 95\%
interval. Holm-adjusted $p$ values cover all primary scenario/heave-horizontal
TFG-versus-shared-QMEKF tests. Both absolute differences and per-seed relative
differences are retained. The aggregate first averages log ratios across
conditions within seed, then constructs a seed-level interval: 200 Hz samples
are never treated as independent replications. Interactions are tested directly:
\begin{equation}
 I_s=(E_{\mathrm{TFG,shared}}-E_{\mathrm{Q,shared}})
      -(E_{\mathrm{TFG,ind}}-E_{\mathrm{Q,ind}}).
\end{equation}
Recovery measurements can be censored when the required settling window is
not reached; missing values and estimator failures remain visible in per-run
results. Failure counts accompany comparisons so selection cannot disappear
inside an average.
The separate transition diagnostic uses trailing 10 s RMS, evaluated each
second, below 1.25 times the post-transition steady RMS for 20 consecutive
evaluations. It measures time from the transition end, with explicit censoring.
The inherited startup convergence statistic instead requires every remaining
30 s rolling RMS to stay below 1.5 times the scored RMS. Outage recovery requires
30 s continuously below the larger of 0.5 degrees and twice the pre-gap RMS.
These relative criteria are reported alongside absolute errors.
An additional replay uses eight pinned vessel-RAO records from release v1.2.3
and eight sensor/initialization seeds per record; inference there is conditional
on each fixed wave realization. The reduced-heave diagnostic was added after
the primary finding, uses 12 paired seeds and is explicitly exploratory.

\section{Computational Cost and Embedded Evidence}
The implementation uses one common TFG core parameterized by a motion policy;
its original two-template-argument interface remains valid. It uses the same
minimal realization and confidence facade as the shared QMEKF. Only the
small spatial process-noise blocks and coordinate adaptation depend on the
motion policy. Prediction, gyro/bias correlations, gain/Joseph calculation,
measurement semantics, group injection and covariance reset are shared.

For $n$ world columns and $N=9+3n$ error states, the two structured
$\Phi P$ passes perform $2N[30+3n(n+3)]$ scalar multiplications and
$2N[21+3n(n+2)]$ additions in their explicit loops. These counts cover the
covariance transport kernel, not matrix discretization, trigonometric functions,
process-noise assembly or measurement updates. They are 4788/3906 for the
21-state TFG and 10368/8910 for the 27-state TFG. This is a structured
$O(N^2n)$ calculation rather than a generic dense matrix product. Reset
transport similarly exploits the attitude-column structure.

\PerformanceResults
Fixed-size member scratch replaces covariance-sized real-time stack arrays.
Eigen's allocation guard exercises prediction, sensor updates and adaptation;
the wrapper allocates its estimator at construction as before. There is no
new heap allocation in the real-time sample path. Double arithmetic in model
commits and spectral factorization is retained for numerical fidelity.
Target measurements use the actual Xtensa compiler for code/data size,
\texttt{sizeof} and compiler stack/callgraph reports. These are build-time
measurements. A static call-chain estimate excludes opaque library and
indirect-call frames and is a lower bound on actual peak stack, not a measured
high-water mark. No connected ESP32-S3 was available: neither on-device
200 Hz deadlines nor actual peak stack have been validated. Host timing cannot
establish either claim. \FirmwareResults

\section{Reproduction, Limitations and Recommendation}
The runner \texttt{tools/tfg\_shared\_modes\_study.py} records the executable
hash, source hashes, exact commands, seed lists, cases, policies and score
windows. Its per-run summaries and statistical tables are compact; large raw
sample streams are regenerated. Seed-one plot traces are explicitly drawing
data. The same peak-preserving helper bounds both SVG and PGF output; all
statistics and transient windows precede drawing decimation. Committed figures
are regenerated by \texttt{tools/tfg\_shared\_modes\_report.py}.

This experiment does not establish sea-trial performance, absolute navigation,
a global convergence theorem, robustness to arbitrary magnetic disturbance,
or embedded deadline compliance. Stationary/marginal process equivalence is
stronger than bitwise equivalence of nonlinear Gaussian finite-correction
implementations. The synthetic directional RAOs and sensor processes cover
specified mismatch modes but cannot replace measured vessel histories.
The inherited motion/bias ambiguity can produce large joint NEES even when
position RMS is small; changing group geometry alone cannot remove a physical
information deficit. Practical recommendations therefore consider consistency,
recovery and computation alongside RMS.
