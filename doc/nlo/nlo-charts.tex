\section*{TimeVarGain NLO, VVR-only, no GNSS, no magnetometer}

This report shows the no-GNSS/no-magnetometer TimeVarGain nonlinear observer simulation.
The observer uses the vertical VVR constraint and IMU measurements only. Yaw is free and is not interpreted as an observable output.

The PLL coarse-frequency smoother advances once per detected period using
that elapsed period. For this broadband displacement channel, its time
constant is 120 seconds; the gain multiplier is 0.5575 and the separate
gain-smoothing time constant remains 30 seconds. The longer coarse horizon
reduces irregular-wave crossing jitter, with a corresponding acquisition lag.
Validation here covers the 5 kn vessel records with slow heading oscillation, not a guarantee of tracking
rapidly changing seas.

\section*{Drift rejection in weak encounter acceleration}

Following seas lower encounter frequency and acceleration without slowing
physical IMU bias drift. A frequency-only bandwidth schedule therefore loses
drift rejection. The adapter now estimates the central variance $V$ of measured
vertical acceleration through the shared period-scaled wave band
($0.5 f$ to $4 f$, subject to its usual physical and sampling guards).
The acceleration is projected using the current attitude, excluding the
translational correction $\xi$. Attitude and frequency estimation remain
coupled to the observer. The shared variance estimator averages four wave
periods, with its existing horizon bounds.

Let $s=0.12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, let $\theta_w$ be the existing clamped
frequency command, and let $\theta_0$ be the configured capture bandwidth
(default 1). Before the existing 30-second smoothing, the command becomes
\[
 w=\frac{1}{1+(V/s^2)^2},\qquad
 \theta_c=\operatorname{clip}
 \left(\sqrt{\theta_w^2+w\theta_0^2},\,0.15,\,1.60\right).
\]
The scale $s$ is empirical; it is neither an estimate of sensor noise nor a
still-water classification. No displacement is suppressed or reset.
Setting \texttt{theta\_accel\_scale\_mps2=0} restores the previous schedule.
The core gains, frequency tracker, output high-pass and startup policy are
retained. Band filtering matters: using broadband acceleration variance
instead causes a substantial small-wave regression with machinery vibration.

Paired zero-speed and 5 kn measurements, held-out sensor seeds, engine
vibration and noise-free controls are recorded in
\texttt{reports/results/encounter\_scaling/nlo\_band*.json} and discussed in
\texttt{docs/encounter-scaling-regressions.md}. The adjustment trades some
noise-free wave fidelity for stronger drift rejection; it is not a universal
optimum. Moving-data JONSWAP and low-wave PM-Stokes regression limits tighten
to 9.48 and 9.17 percent of nominal $H_s$, respectively; the other PM-Stokes
limit remains 8.19 percent.
