\begin{abstract}
Low-frequency drift is a dominant failure mode of acceleration-only marine
motion estimation, while an overly strong zero-position constraint suppresses
the long-period motion being measured.  This paper studies an integral-state
regularizer: augment the inertial chain with $S=\int p\,dt$ and apply a virtual
$S=0$ observation whose covariance varies with sea state.  The virtual
observation is a regularizer rather than a physical sensor.  OU similarity
identifies $\sigma_a\tau^3$ as the natural coordinate scale of the third
integral, but that dimensional statement does not select the Kalman
regularization strength.  A reduced drift--distortion calculation instead gives
$r_S^\star\propto q_{\rm eff}^{1/14}m_{-4}^{3/7}T_S^{-1/2}$ and, for a
self-similar period-scaled sea, the unclipped schedule
$r_S^\star\propto\sigma_a^{6/7}\tau^{41/14}$.  Exact spectral evaluation on
the reference sea records reproduces the reduced exponents and determines the
SpectralMSE coefficient used by the implementation.  The reduction also shows
why large horizontal drift intensity alone does not imply proportionally
stronger horizontal regularization.  The result is a current-law design account
rather than a historical comparison of superseded regularizer schedules.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Acceleration-only motion estimation contains an unavoidable tension.  Repeated
integration preserves physical displacement in the wave band, but small
specific-force errors create rapidly growing low-frequency velocity and
position error.  Marine heave estimators therefore commonly introduce
high-pass structure, model-based restoring dynamics, or virtual measurements in
addition to the IMU
\cite{Kuechler2011_HeaveAcceleration,Auestad2013_HeaveStrapdownIMU,Reis2023_DiscreteKalmanHeave}.
For low-cost IMUs the problem is not confined to the vertical axis: attitude
error leaks gravity into horizontal specific force and residual horizontal
accelerometer bias is weakly observable without an external position or
velocity reference \cite{YurovskyKudinov2025_IMUwaves}.

The estimator considered here uses the augmented linear chain
\begin{equation}
 \dot v=a,\qquad \dot p=v,\qquad \dot S=p,
 \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
and periodically applies
\begin{equation}
 y_S=0=S+n_S,\qquad n_S\sim\mathcal N(0,R_S),
 \qquad R_S=r_S^2.
 \label{eq:pseudo}
\end{equation}
The observation $S=0$ is not physically true at each update.  It expresses the
long-time belief that bounded oscillatory motion should not acquire an
unbounded third integral.  The design question is therefore not merely how $S$
scales dimensionally, but how strongly the false zero should be trusted while
preserving real low-frequency wave displacement.

The contributions are: (i) an OU similarity statement for the third-integral
coordinate; (ii) the reduced regularization bandwidth produced by a periodic
$S=0$ update; (iii) a drift--distortion MSE optimum in a stated scalar model;
(iv) exact spectral evaluation of that optimum and its coefficient; and (v) an
axis-dependent interpretation showing why drift intensity and desired motion
must both enter any anisotropic extension.  Historical tuning-law comparisons
are intentionally outside the scope of this paper.

\section{Integral-State Regularization Model}
\label{sec:model}

For one translational axis, let the latent motion acceleration follow a
stationary OU model
\begin{equation}
 da=-\frac{1}{\tau}a\,dt
 +\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma_a^2}{\tau}}\,dW_t,
 \qquad \Var[a]=\sigma_a^2,
 \label{eq:ou}
\end{equation}
with the chain in Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain}.  OU acceleration provides a finite
correlation time and an exact discrete transition while remaining a compact
colored-acceleration prior \cite{UhlenbeckOrnstein1930_OU}.  The physical wave
spectrum need not be OU; the estimator uses $(\tau,\sigma_a)$ as local
sea-state prior parameters.

The periodic $S=0$ update is applied every $T_S$.  In three dimensions,
\begin{equation}
 \mat R_S=\diag(r_{S,x}^2,r_{S,y}^2,r_{S,z}^2).
 \label{eq:RS3}
\end{equation}
Reducing $R_S$ increases drift-anchor authority, but because the true integral
state is generally nonzero it also increases distortion of wanted motion.  A
useful schedule must balance those two effects.

\section{OU Similarity and the Third-Integral Coordinate Scale}
\label{sec:similarity}

\begin{theorem}[OU similarity scale of the third integral]
\label{thm:similarity}
Under Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ou} and \eqref{eq:chain}, define
\begin{equation}
 \bar t=\frac{t}{\tau},\quad
 \bar a=\frac{a}{\sigma_a},\quad
 \bar v=\frac{v}{\sigma_a\tau},\quad
 \bar p=\frac{p}{\sigma_a\tau^2},\quad
 \bar S=\frac{S}{\sigma_a\tau^3}.
 \label{eq:nondim}
\end{equation}
Then the normalized stochastic dynamics contain neither $\sigma_a$ nor $\tau$.
Consequently the natural coordinate scales of $(a,v,p,S)$ are
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_a,\qquad \sigma_a\tau,\qquad
 \sigma_a\tau^2,\qquad \boxed{\sigma_a\tau^3}.
 \label{eq:natural-scales}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $t=\tau\bar t$ and use Brownian scaling
$dW_t=\sqrt{\tau}\,d\bar W_{\bar t}$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:ou} becomes
$d\bar a=-\bar a\,d\bar t+\sqrt2\,d\bar W_{\bar t}$.  Successive integrations
give $d\bar v/d\bar t=\bar a$, $d\bar p/d\bar t=\bar v$, and
$d\bar S/d\bar t=\bar p$.
\end{proof}

The boxed term is a \emph{coordinate-scale statement}, not an adaptation law.
It establishes the dimensions and similarity transformation of the integral
state but does not determine how a deliberately mis-specified zero observation
should trade drift rejection against physical-signal distortion.

\section{Reduced Regularization Bandwidth}
\label{sec:bandwidth}

At frequencies well below the posterior acceleration correlation rate, suppose
the residual acceleration error is approximated by white noise with two-sided
intensity $q_{\rm eff}$.  Reorder the integrated states as
$x_d=[S,p,v]^T$ and approximate periodic $S$ updates by a continuous
observation with noise density
\begin{equation}
 r_{S,c}=R_ST_S.
 \label{eq:rsc}
\end{equation}
The corresponding continuous algebraic Riccati equation gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 K_d=\begin{bmatrix}2\omega_R&2\omega_R^2&\omega_R^3\end{bmatrix}^{T},
 \qquad
 \omega_R=\left(\frac{q_{\rm eff}}{R_ST_S}\right)^{1/6}.}
 \label{eq:omegaR}
\end{equation}
The closed-loop polynomial is
\begin{equation}
 s^3+2\omega_Rs^2+2\omega_R^2s+\omega_R^3
 =(s+\omega_R)(s^2+\omega_Rs+\omega_R^2).
 \label{eq:poles}
\end{equation}
Thus $R_S$ and the update cadence together set a low-frequency correction
corner through a sixth-root dependence.

\section{Reduced Physical-MSE Optimum}
\label{sec:reduced-mse}

Let $m_{-4}$ denote the negative fourth moment of the desired displacement
spectrum over the wave band,
\begin{equation}
 m_{-4}=\int_{\Omega_{\rm wave}}
 \frac{S_p^{\rm true}(\omega)}{\omega^4}\,d\omega.
 \label{eq:m4}
\end{equation}
For the reduced observer of Eq.~\eqref{eq:omegaR}, residual acceleration error
produces a drift term that decreases as $\omega_R^{-3}$, while leakage of the
nonphysical zero constraint into desired displacement grows as
$\omega_R^4$ when the wave band lies above the correction corner.

\begin{theorem}[Reduced drift--distortion optimum]
\label{thm:reduced-mse}
Under the scalar continuous approximation above, the leading physical position
MSE is
\begin{equation}
 J_{\rm red}(\omega_R)
 =\frac{3q_{\rm eff}}{2\omega_R^3}
 +4m_{-4}\omega_R^4.
 \label{eq:Jred}
\end{equation}
Its unique positive minimizer satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{(\omega_R^\star)^7
 =\frac{9}{32}\frac{q_{\rm eff}}{m_{-4}}},
 \label{eq:wopt}
\end{equation}
and, using Eq.~\eqref{eq:omegaR},
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{r_S^\star\propto
 q_{\rm eff}^{1/14}m_{-4}^{3/7}T_S^{-1/2}.}
 \label{eq:rsopt}
\end{equation}
The minimized objective scales as
\begin{equation}
 J_{\rm red}^\star\propto q_{\rm eff}^{4/7}m_{-4}^{3/7}.
 \label{eq:jopt-scaling}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate Eq.~\eqref{eq:Jred} with respect to $\omega_R$.  Setting
$-(9/2)q_{\rm eff}\omega_R^{-4}+16m_{-4}\omega_R^3=0$ gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:wopt}.  Substitution into
$R_S=q_{\rm eff}/(\omega_R^6T_S)$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:rsopt}; substituting the
same optimum into either balanced term yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:jopt-scaling}.
\end{proof}

The theorem states the optimum of $J_{\rm red}$, not of the complete MEKF.  Its
value is mechanistic: it separates low-frequency error intensity from physical
wave content and shows their very different logarithmic sensitivities.

For a fixed-shape, period-scaled sea,
\begin{equation}
 m_{-4}\propto\sigma_a^2\tau^8.
 \label{eq:m4-similarity}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
 r_S^\star\propto q_{\rm eff}^{1/14}
 \sigma_a^{6/7}\tau^{24/7}T_S^{-1/2}.
 \label{eq:rs-self-similar}
\end{equation}
If $q_{\rm eff}$ is amplitude-independent to leading order in the strong
acceleration-observation regime and $T_S\propto\tau$, then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{r_S^\star\propto\sigma_a^{6/7}\tau^{41/14}.}
 \label{eq:rs-unclipped}
\end{equation}

\section{Exact Spectral Evaluation}
\label{sec:exact}

The high-separation approximation can be checked without introducing another
regularizer law.  Retaining the exact reconstruction error gives
\begin{equation}
J_{\rm wave}(\omega_R)
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{1+4x^2}{1+x^6}\,S_\eta^{(2)}(\omega)\,d\omega,
\qquad x=\frac{\omega}{\omega_R},
\label{eq:exact-wave-cost}
\end{equation}
with total cost $J=3q_{\rm eff}/(2\omega_R^3)+J_{\rm wave}$.  Exact numerical
minimization on the eight reference spectra gives the local exponents in

The exact exponents reproduce $(6/7,41/14)$ to three significant figures over

that agrees with direct numerical integration of the reference spectra to

Writing the implemented current law as
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
r_{S,\star}=C_J q_{\rm eff}^{1/14}
 \widehat\sigma_{a,B}^{\,6/7}
 \tau_\star^{24/7}T_{S,\star}^{-1/2},
 \qquad C_J=0.0538,}
\label{eq:deployed-spectral-mse}
\end{equation}
the coefficient $C_J$ collects the normalized spectral moment and constants of
the reduced optimization.  Its reference-spectrum evaluation yields
$C_J\simeq0.0538$; no historical schedule is required as a numerical anchor.

\section{Current Sea-State Schedule}
\label{sec:schedule}

The complete adaptation layer has three separate mappings,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\tau_\star&=c_\tau T_{\rm sea},\\
\sigma_{aw,\star}&=c_\sigma\widehat\sigma_{a,B},\\
T_{S,\star}&=\clip(c_T\tau_\star,T_{S,\min},T_{S,\max}),\\
r_{S,\star}&=C_J q_{\rm eff}^{1/14}
 \widehat\sigma_{a,B}^{\,6/7}
 \tau_\star^{24/7}T_{S,\star}^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:current-schedule}
\end{equation}
The first two quantities define the approximate OU prior; the third line sets
how often the integral regularizer is applied; the fourth independently sets
its strength by the reduced physical-MSE criterion.  This separation is
important because the physical acceleration statistic belongs to the sea while
$q_{\rm eff}$ describes the posterior low-frequency error that the regularizer
must reject.

\section{Where the Analytical Logic Stops}
\label{sec:limits}

The reduced calculation does not turn the virtual observation into a physical
measurement.  $R_S$ is a design covariance, and in the implementation no random
$n_S$ is generated: the filter receives the deterministic virtual value zero.
Consequently the filter-internal Riccati covariance and the actual
estimation-error covariance need not coincide when the virtual observation is
mis-specified.  The complete estimator also contains attitude-to-gravity
leakage, gyro and accelerometer bias states, cross-covariances, discrete
pseudo-updates, schedule smoothing, and model mismatch that are absent from the
scalar reduction.

The reduced theorem therefore supplies a physically interpretable schedule and
a coefficient construction, not a proof of complete-filter optimality.  Full-
estimator experiments remain the validation authority for the complete MEKF,
and external physical validation remains necessary before extrapolating beyond
the tested simulator, spectra, sensor model, and operating envelope.

\section{Axis Dependence and Isotropic Deployment}
\label{sec:axes}

For axis $i$, the reduced optimum is
\begin{equation}
 r_{S,i}^\star\propto
 q_i^{1/14}m_{-4,i}^{3/7}T_S^{-1/2}.
 \label{eq:axis-current}
\end{equation}
The tested stationary records give the drift-band diagnostic
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{q_x/q_z\simeq65.4,\qquad q_y/q_z\simeq102.3.}
 \label{eq:q-horizontal-ratios}
\end{equation}
These large ratios are consistent with first-order horizontal gravity leakage
$\varepsilon_{a,x}\simeq g\,\delta\theta_y$ and
$\varepsilon_{a,y}\simeq-g\,\delta\theta_x$, together with weaker horizontal
bias observability.  They do not imply regularizer ratios of the same size:
$q_i$ enters only through $q_i^{1/14}$, while the desired-motion spectral moment
enters through $m_{-4,i}^{3/7}$, six times the logarithmic sensitivity.

The desired horizontal spectra are heading-dependent projections of the orbital
field.  Sparse directional simulations therefore cannot support a universal
numerical $x/y$ optimum.  Isotropic integral regularization therefore performs
adequately over the tested directional ensemble without asserting that the
three physical channels are isotropic.  A more direct horizontal improvement is
to reduce $q_{\rm eff,H}$ itself through better tilt estimation, accelerometer-
bias observability, GNSS, or another horizontal reference.  If reliable
axis-specific desired-motion spectra and aiding become available,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:axis-current} gives the appropriate extension.

\section{Conclusion}

The third integral of an OU acceleration process has the natural coordinate
scale $\sigma_a\tau^3$, but dimensional similarity alone does not determine the
strength of a false zero observation.  Balancing low-frequency drift against
physical-wave distortion instead gives the reduced optimum
$r_S^\star\propto q_{\rm eff}^{1/14}m_{-4}^{3/7}T_S^{-1/2}$ and the
self-similar unclipped law
$r_S^\star\propto\sigma_a^{6/7}\tau^{41/14}$.  Exact evaluation on the
reference spectra supports the reduced exponents and supplies the present
SpectralMSE coefficient.  The same analysis explains why large horizontal
drift intensity has only weak direct leverage on the regularizer optimum.  The
remaining problem is to quantify how the full attitude/bias-coupled discrete
MEKF modifies the reduced physical-MSE result and to validate that prediction
against external motion reference data.
