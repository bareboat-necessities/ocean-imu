\begin{abstract}
We present an adaptive quaternion multiplicative EKF for three-dimensional
marine inertial wave-motion estimation.  The 21-state estimator couples attitude
and sensor biases with an OU-driven translational chain comprising velocity,
oscillatory displacement, integral displacement, and latent world acceleration.
A zero pseudo-measurement on the integral state provides low-frequency
regularization.  The core model admits analytic discrete-time propagation, and
the normal Live recursion is locally input-to-state stable under explicit
bounded-schedule, observability, covariance, and no-reset assumptions.  The
measurement-only startup observer additionally gives an almost-global,
semiglobal practical attitude-capture extension: uniformly on every compact
startup set contained in its desired almost-global domain, a quality-gated
handoff enters the certified Live basin under explicit bounded-disturbance and
handoff conditions.
Sea-state adaptation is implemented as an exogenous measurement-only layer that
estimates wave period and band-limited acceleration variance and schedules the
OU prior and integral regularizer.  A paired ten-seed study spans four
stationary JONSWAP seas, four PM--Stokes seas, and a controlled transition.  For
the stationary JONSWAP primary endpoint, normalized vertical RMS error is
$\OUValidationOUIIINormalizedMean\pm\OUValidationOUIIINormalizedStd\%$ of
$H_s$ for OU--III versus
$\OUValidationOUIINormalizedMean\pm\OUValidationOUIINormalizedStd\%$ for
OU--II, with a paired difference of $\OUValidationNormalizedDifference$
percentage points and bootstrap 95\% interval
$[\OUValidationNormalizedDifferenceLow,\OUValidationNormalizedDifferenceHigh]$.
Ablations attribute most of the adaptive benefit to integral-state
regularization.  Results remain simulation based and do not establish global
navigation, thermal-calibration, or embedded resource performance.
\end{abstract}
