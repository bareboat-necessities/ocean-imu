\begin{abstract}
The standalone shared-modes QMEKF (CSM) gives the CARMA3 filter one
complexified CARMA(4,1) mode per axis with a Hermitian excitation estimated
from the IMU, and adds a coupling confidence that withdraws the coupling,
preserving positivity of the covariance, when its evidence fails. In a
paired simulation study (18 seas, 50 seeds; robustness 4 seas, 30 seeds)
against the CARMA3 baseline at the same wave excitation, CSM lowers the
displacement error pooled over 16 wave seas by 4.1\,\%, 1.8\,\% and 4.5\,\%
(north, east, heave; 95\,\% intervals within $\pm0.3$ points). It is
significantly better on 13--15 of the 16 seas and loses only while
manoeuvring (6.8\,\% heave). Every deliberately wrong coupling prior is
rejected: with the confidence these runs equal the independent-axis
fallback, without it they lose up to 131\,\% with NEES up to 134. The
confidence is conservative and gives away part of the research filter's
gain on two-system seas. The Evidence excitation leaves the motion
covariance near zero before waves arrive, which makes NEES at wave onset
very large for the baseline and CSM alike. CSM costs \SI{6.6}{\micro s} per
update on the host and a \SI{15.8}{kB} filter object on the ESP32-S3. It
remains an experimental filter.
\end{abstract}

\section{Experimental Objectives}
The standalone shared-modes QMEKF (\texttt{KalmanCarmaSharedModes}, CSM)
promotes Option C of the coupled-prior study: one complexified CARMA(4,1)
mode per axis, driven by a Hermitian excitation estimated from the filter's
own acceleration, plus a coupling confidence that withdraws the coupling,
with a positivity-preserving covariance decoupling, when the evidence fails.
The companion article derives the model. This one asks three questions.
(i) Does CSM reproduce the validated research filter when its confidence is
disabled? (ii) What does the confidence change on the 18 seas of the study,
and in particular on the two the research filter lost (manoeuvring and wave
onset/cessation)? (iii) Does it prevent catastrophic overconfidence when the
coupling prior is wrong, with bounded errors and reasonable uncertainty?
Results of the coupled-prior study are labelled as previously established;
everything else was produced for this article.

\section{Simulation Framework}
\texttt{kalman\_carma\_shared\_modes-sim} has two modes. The \emph{dataset
replay} runs the eight scored records of the pinned dataset through the same
\texttt{W3dSimulationRunner}, seeds, sensor model and adapter configuration
as the CARMA3 replay, with regression bars. The \emph{Monte Carlo mode} runs
the carma3\_bench engine: synthetic directional seas through the benchmark
hull's RAOs, seeded IMU and magnetometer streams, and the same metrics, with
the CSM filters registered in front of the bench's own. Every filter of a row
(the CARMA3 baseline A41 and its Evidence-excitation variant A41@C, the
research filter SHM:est, CSM and its fallback model CSM:off) sees the same
truth and the same sensor stream.

\section{Wave-Data Provenance}
\InputPart{../simulation-dataset.tex-part}
The Monte Carlo seas are the 18 scenarios of the coupled-prior study
(\texttt{tests/kalman\_carma/CarmaBenchTruth.h}): regular waves, PM, JONSWAP
long-crested and spread, swell, short wind waves, mixed, bimodal and
crossing seas, calm water, a still IMU, direction and period changes, onset
and cessation, heading changes, manoeuvring, a very low sea, and magnetic
disturbances with calibration residuals. Ground-truth position, velocity,
acceleration, direction and RAO are used for scoring only. The robustness
injections build their deliberately wrong priors from the true wave
acceleration, and enter the filter as an external prior that it must reject
from its own data.

\section{Sensor-Noise Assumptions}
Accelerometer \SI{1.5}{mg} white with turn-on bias up to \SI{8}{mg} and a
random walk; gyroscope \SI{0.0016}{rad/s} white with bias up to
\SI{0.05}{\degree/s}; magnetometer \SI{0.8}{\micro T} at \SI{25}{Hz} with
hard and soft iron, and in one scenario periodic \SI{15}{\micro T}
disturbances with scale and misalignment residuals; \SI{200}{Hz} with timing
jitter where stated. These are the CARMA3 benchmark's, unchanged.

\section{Baseline CARMA3 Configuration}
A41: the experimental CARMA3 QMEKF with CARMA(4,1) C41J on all axes (21
states), behind the unchanged CARMA3 wrapper (OU-III's startup, magnetic
acquisition, tuner and \SI{0.1}{s} commits). It is verified bit-identical to
the code before the coupled-prior work.

\section{Shared-Mode Configuration}
CSM: 33 states, the estimated coupling (\SI{240}{s} horizon, band
$0.15f_{op}$, both screened on seeds 1001--1020 by the coupled-prior study)
and the coupling confidence with its a-priori defaults (consistency $D^2$
thresholds 6/16, SNR 10--100, yaw rate \SI{3}{\degree/s}, sea change
$0.01$/s, holds \SI{30}{s}, NIS ratio 2/1.5, $\tau=3/60$\,s). CSM uses the
Evidence wave excitation of the CARMA3 wrapper (noise-debiased band
evidence, the mode the AtomS3R sketches deploy); A41 and SHM:est keep the
Legacy excitation of the coupled-prior study, and A41@C is A41 with
Evidence excitation, so CSM against A41@C isolates the shared modes.
CSM:noconf is CSM with the confidence disabled. With the excitation paired
(Legacy) it reproduces SHM:est: bit-identical in the unit test, and equal to
float rounding ($5\times10^{-5}$) in the benchmark rows, where the two
objects' memory layouts differ. CSM:off is the coupling switched off, the
independent-axis law of the complexified realization.

\section{Monte Carlo Methodology}
Every row runs one scenario and seed (1--50 final, 1--30 robustness, 1--10
spectra), \SI{1200}{s} unless the scenario says otherwise, scored from
\SI{300}{s} against the wave coordinate. No parameter was chosen on these
seeds.

\section{Statistical Methods}
Changes are $1-\overline{\mathrm{RMS}}_{\mathrm{cand}}/\overline{\mathrm{RMS}}_{\mathrm{A41}}$
over seeds, with 95\,\% intervals from a paired bootstrap that resamples whole
realizations (seeds), never the autocorrelated \SI{200}{Hz} samples. NEES is
that of $[\vct p,\vct v]$ in the filter's own coordinates. Its \emph{mean}
should equal 6 for a consistent filter; the median of a $\chi^2_6$ variable
is 5.35, so both are reported and are not compared with each other. NIS is
the accelerometer innovation's (3 dof). Its absolute level reflects the
inflated measurement noise, so innovation whiteness and cross-axis
correlation are reported as well.

\section{Position and Velocity Results}
\InputPart{csm-results-generated.tex-part}
Table~\ref{tab:final} lists every sea against A41 (Legacy excitation), the
historical baseline. Like for like, against A41@C, CSM lowers the error
pooled over the 16 wave seas by 4.1\,\%, 1.8\,\% and 4.5\,\% ($x$, $y$, $z$;
intervals $[+3.9,+4.3]$, $[+1.5,+2.1]$, $[+4.4,+4.7]$). It is significantly
better on 15, 15 and 13 of the 16 seas, most on long-period swell (+19\,\%,
+18\,\% heave) and the regular wave (+11\,\%, +10\,\%). It is worse on
manoeuvring ($-6.8$\,\% heave), short-period wind waves ($-0.8$\,\%) and the
\SI{5}{cm} sea ($-0.3$\,\% north). The Evidence excitation alone (A41@C
against A41) contributes $+5.4$, $+3.8$ and $+10.0$\,\%: it removes the
\SIrange{52}{61}{mm} phantom displacement of calm water, gains 64--70\,\% at
wave onset and cessation and 27\,\% on swell heave, and loses 25\,\%
horizontally on the \SI{5}{cm} sea. The research filter SHM:est gains
$+6.0$, $+2.6$ and $+6.0$\,\% against A41 at its own (Legacy) excitation.
CSM keeps less of that because its confidence withdraws the coupling on
two-system seas. In exchange it halves the manoeuvring loss ($-6.3$ against
$-12.7$\,\% heave) and removes the onset loss. CSM:off is within 0.7 points
of A41@C, the cost of the complexified realization under adaptation. On the
dataset replay CSM's 3-D error is below CSM:off's on all eight records,
by 2--10\,\%. CSM never failed; A41 tripped the bench's divergence check
($|e|>\SI{10}{m}$) on one seed in calm water, still IMU and onset.

The energy-weighted relative error of the displacement cross-spectral
matrix against the truth's (0.05--1.5\,Hz, 10 seeds) is 0.084 for A41,

Figures~\ref{fig:dir} and~\ref{fig:onoff} show the confidence through a
wave-direction change and through wave onset and cessation. After the onset
at \SI{400}{s} the confidence stays at zero for about \SI{50}{s} and then
rises with its \SI{60}{s} time constant, so the coupling is applied from
about \SI{450}{s}. After the cessation it falls sharply at about
\SI{1005}{s}. It then recovers while the vertical excitation is near zero,
where the coupling has no effect on the prior. Over the record the heave
error is 3.2\,\% below A41@C's, where SHM:est loses 3.5\,\% against A41. On the two-system seas the
single shared mode cannot represent both systems. The short-horizon monitor
disagrees with the prior, and the consistency test withdraws most of the
coupling: the mean applied $x$--$z$ coherence is 0.09 for CSM against 0.47
for SHM:est on two systems at different frequencies, and the $y$--$z$
coherence on the mixed sea is 0.06 against 0.38. This is the main source of
the gap to the research filter.

\section{Coupling Estimation Accuracy}
The coupling estimated from the IMU gives within 0.3 points of an external
prior built from the true wave acceleration on the three robustness seas
(spread JONSWAP: $+1.7$/$+5.0$\,\% horizontal/heave against $+1.8$/$+4.9$\,\%;
same seeds). The estimated excitation phase is stable across seeds:
$x$--$z$ \SI{124\pm3}{\degree} on JONSWAP, \SI{122\pm3}{\degree} on PM and
\SI{96\pm7}{\degree} on swell, with mean applied $x$--$z$ coherence 0.13,
0.12 and 0.33.

Falsified external priors enter the filter, which must reject them from its
own data (Fig.~\ref{fig:rob}). With the confidence, every falsified prior --
reversed, turned \SI{90}{\degree}, phase $+\SI{45}{\degree}$, doubled
amplitude -- gives exactly the coupling-off result on all three seas: the
coupling was never applied. Without it, the reversed and turned priors lose
4--30\,\% and the doubled amplitude up to 131\,\% (spread JONSWAP heave), with
mean NEES up to 134 (turned prior, mixed sea). The protection has a price: the confidence also
rejects a \SI{45}{\degree} phase error that would still gain about 3\,\%, and
on the mixed sea it withdraws the true coupling ($+2.0$/$+4.1$\,\% against
$+8.5$/$+13.8$\,\% without the confidence). Through heading zigzags the
yaw-rate hold keeps the applied coherence at 0.03 (SHM:est 0.13): CSM gains
$+0.4$/$+2.4$\,\% where the unprotected filter gains $+1.8$/$+5.1$\,\%. The
hold does protect a coupling frozen in the keel frame ($+1.1$ against
$-1.1$\,\% heave).

\section{NEES/NIS Consistency}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\ChartFigure{consistency_nees_nis.pgf}
\caption{NEES of $[\vct p,\vct v]$ (mean o, median x; 6 = consistent mean)
and mean accelerometer NIS per sea.}
\label{fig:nees}
\end{figure}
CSM's mean NEES is at or below A41@C's on every sea except wave onset:
4.8--6.5 on the irregular single-system seas, 3.5--3.7 on the very low sea
and wind waves. Every model is overconfident on the regular wave (CSM 12.8),
the period change (9.5), the two-peak seas (two systems 9.0, mixed 57.9: one
CARMA mode cannot represent two spectral peaks) and manoeuvring (59.3). Wave
onset is the exception. In Evidence mode, with no wave evidence the
excitation is zero and the motion covariance collapses (the heave standard
deviation is \SI{0.000}{mm} before onset). When waves start, the filter
carries a \SI{10}{mm} heave error against that covariance. The resulting NEES
is about $10^4$ for A41@C, CSM and CSM:off alike, against 5.7 for Legacy A41.
The position-interval coverage stays at 0.96--0.97, and the error is far
below Legacy's (\SI{10.5}{mm} against \SI{100}{mm} over \SIrange{300}{400}{s}).

\section{Embedded Computational Feasibility}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\ChartFigure{cost_time_memory.pgf}
\caption{Host update time and ESP32-S3 code size, QMEKF object size and
largest stack frame.}
\label{fig:cost}
\end{figure}
On the host CSM's update takes \SI{6.6}{\micro s} on average
(\SI{32.9}{\micro s} at the 99th percentile) against \SI{3.2}{\micro s}
(\SI{7.0}{\micro s}) for A41; the confidence adds \SI{0.2}{\micro s} over
SHM:est. On the ESP32-S3 (Xtensa GCC 14.2.0, \texttt{-Os}) the code grows
from \SI{173}{kB} to \SI{196}{kB}, the QMEKF object from \SI{6.1}{kB} to
\SI{15.8}{kB} (the $33\times33$ covariance), the largest stack frame from
\SI{1.3}{kB} to \SI{2.5}{kB}, and the update-path stack from \SI{4.8}{kB} to
\SI{4.9}{kB}. The AtomS3R sketch \texttt{atomS3R\_ins\_carma\_shared\_modes}
builds for the target; it is not hardware evidence.

\section{Limitations}
All results are simulation: the hull, response operators and sensor model
are the CARMA3 benchmark's, and no hardware or field data is used. The
Evidence-mode covariance collapse at wave onset belongs to the excitation
mode the AtomS3R sketches deploy, not to the coupling, and is not changed
here. The confidence is conservative: it gives away 6--11 points on
two-system seas and about 3 through heading changes. CSM still loses 6.8\,\%
heave while manoeuvring, and Evidence excitation costs 25\,\% horizontally on
a \SI{5}{cm} sea (\SI{0.8}{mm}).

\section{Conclusions}
(i) With the confidence disabled and the excitation paired, CSM reproduces
the research filter. (ii) The confidence removes the research filter's
losses at wave onset and halves the manoeuvring loss. It gives away part of
the gain on two-system seas, leaving CSM $+4.1$/$+1.8$/$+4.5$\,\% better than
the baseline at the same excitation. (iii) It rejects every falsified
coupling prior, so a wrong prior costs nothing instead of up to 131\,\%.
CSM stays an experimental filter. Two open items remain: the Evidence-mode
covariance at wave onset, and a less conservative consistency test for
two-system seas.
