# Startup transient under engine noise: NLO, TFG, PII, OU-II, OU-III

Generated by `tools/startup_transient_study.py` from the pinned
oceanography-waves-lib v1.2.1 vessel records. It covers all 20 records: Gerstner,
cnoidal, Fenton, JONSWAP, and PM-Stokes at Hs = 0.27, 1.5, 4, and 8.5 m. Every
family runs in its deployed simulator configuration. The engine arm uses the
nominal diesel vibration (2400 rpm, 0.60 m/s² hull RMS, 80 Hz sensor bandwidth),
and an engine-off arm of the same replay serves as the control. Each record is
replayed in full so that each filter's own steady-state error (300–1200 s) is
known. The charts and transient metrics cover the first 180 s.

```
make build
python3 tools/sim_dataset.py --archive sim-data-files-vessel-rao-28ft.zip --dest <data-dir>
python3 tools/startup_transient_study.py --data-dir <data-dir> --work-dir <scratch>
```

## Files

- `<wave>_H<hs>_*.png`: one chart per record, engine on. The left column shows x/y/z
  displacement estimates against the truth, and the right column shows the errors.
  The y-limits cover the truth and the settled errors, so larger startup
  excursions are clipped. PII is vertical-only.
- `settle_own.png`: time until the 10 s rolling error RMS stays below 2× the
  filter's own steady-state error. This measures how long the startup oscillation lasts.
- `settle_usable.png`: time until the 10 s rolling error RMS stays below 50 % of
  the truth's rolling RMS. This measures when the output becomes a usable displacement.
- In both settle charts, bars are the engine arm and black ticks mark engine off.
  A value of 180 means the criterion was not met within 3 min.
- `startup_transient.csv`: one row per arm × family × record × axis. It holds
  `settle_own_s`, `settle_usable_s`, `peak_err_m`, `peak_t_s`, `ss_err_rms_m`,
  `ss_ref_rms_m`, and `ss_ratio`.

For irregular seas only (JONSWAP and PM-Stokes, engine on), the median settle_usable
is TFG 86 s, OU-III 141 s, OU-II 155 s, PII 164 s, and NLO 180 s.

## What the charts show

- **OU-II, OU-III, TFG** output zero displacement for about the first 35 s, while
  startup and capture run. After that, z does not oscillate around the truth. It
  drifts into a large one-sided offset: negative for OU-II and OU-III, typically
  1–5 m on Hs 1.5–8.5 m. The offset holds until about 130–140 s and then collapses
  within about 10 s. TFG shows a shorter positive or negative excursion around
  60–90 s and is usually settled by about 90–100 s. Horizontal x/y from these
  three families locks onto the truth phase within 10–20 s of release. The
  remaining x overshoot decays over 1–2 min.
- **NLO** tracks z from about 2 s and has the smallest startup peaks. It takes
  longest to reach its own steady state, and the engine lengthens that (median
  100 → 162 s). Its x/y is not a usable displacement at any time: the estimate
  wanders by metres, and the steady-state error is 2–4× the truth RMS.
- **PII** (vertical only) tracks z from the start with a low-frequency bias that
  decays over about 60 s. The engine barely changes it (median peak 1.7 m either
  way).
- **Engine vs. off.** For OU-II, OU-III, and TFG, the engine arm has *smaller*
  z startup peaks than engine-off (OU-II 9.6 → 2.6 m) and settles sooner. The
  likely cause is that their front-end vibration guards engage under engine
  noise and slow the early adaptation. This study has not isolated that effect
  (see `tools/ou3_engine_noise_mitigation.py`). The engine lengthens NLO's z
  settling.
- **Cnoidal.** On cnoidal records, every family has a steady-state z error of about
  1× the truth RMS (TFG 0.46×, OU-III 1.8×), with the engine on or off. The
  cnoidal truth is zero-mean, and the cause has not been investigated here.
  Settle times against the filter's own steady state are not meaningful on
  these records.
- **y on head seas.** On the A = 0° records (Gerstner, cnoidal, Fenton), the true
  sway is essentially zero, so y ratios and usable-settle times reflect only noise.
